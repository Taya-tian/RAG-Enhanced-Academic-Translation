9. Husband and wife become the main axis, and the affection between the sexes is the force that binds them together.

10. In my book *American Character*, I once used the term “fortress of life” to describe it.

11. “The one at home” may refer to one’s wife alone; “the family gate” may refer to a large number of uncles, cousins, and nephews; “our own people” may include anyone one wishes to draw into one’s circle in order to express intimacy. The range of “our own people” can expand or contract according to time and place; it can grow so large as to be beyond counting—truly, all under Heaven can become one family.

12. The basic structure of Chinese rural society is what I have called a “differential mode of association”: a network formed by one strand after another of private relationships. This pattern differs from the modern Western “group mode of association.”

13. But from my present point of view, this seems not quite appropriate. It would be more accurate to call the basic social group in Chinese rural society a “small lineage-family.”

14. Structurally, the lineage-family includes the family; the smallest lineage-family may even be equivalent to a family. This is because the basis of the kinship structure is the parent-child relationship—the triangle of father, mother, and child.

15. In anthropological terms, a clan is an organization for carrying on undertakings; when further enlarged, it may become a tribe.

16. My hypothesis is that Chinese rural society adopted the differential mode of association, using the ethical relations of kinship to organize social groups and manage various undertakings, so that the basic family became clan-like in character.

17. The Chinese family is an organization for undertakings, and the size of the family is determined by the size of those undertakings.

18. Since our family is a continuing community organized around undertakings, its main axis lies between father and son, and between mother-in-law and daughter-in-law. It is vertical, not horizontal.

19. I use this metaphor in order to make more concrete a pattern of interpersonal relations in social life. We may call it the group mode of association.

20. The modern pao-chia system belongs to the group mode of association, but it is completely incompatible with the traditional structure.

21. The network formed through procreation and marriage can be extended outward without limit, including people of the past, the present, and the future. The common saying “one cousinship extends three thousand li” expresses just this idea; indeed, “three thousand li” merely suggests the vastness of its reach.

22. In the kinship system, we all have parents, but my parents are not your parents. To go a step further, there are no two people in the world whose recognized kin can be exactly the same. Two brothers may of course have the same parents, but each has his own wife and children.

23. This is much like our circle of kinship. In the Jia family’s Grand View Garden, there could live Lin Daiyu, a cousin through the father’s sister, and Xue Baochai, a cousin through the mother’s sister; later there were still more—Baoqin, Xiuyan, and others. Anyone who could be connected as a relative could be included. But when power and fortune changed, “when the tree falls, the monkeys scatter,” and the circle shrank back into a small cluster.

24. Taking the “self” as the center, like a stone thrown into water, the social relations formed with others are not like members of a group all standing on the same plane. They are more like ripples on the surface of water, spreading outward circle by circle; the farther they spread, the more distant and thinner they become.

25. *Lun* emphasizes distinctions. In the “Ji Tong” chapter of the *Book of Rites*, the ten *lun*—spirits and men, ruler and subject, father and son, noble and lowly, close and distant kin, rank and reward, husband and wife, affairs of government, elder and younger, superior and inferior—all refer to graded differences. “Not losing one’s *lun*” means distinguishing father and son, near and far, intimate and distant. *Lun* is an order of differentiated ranks.

26. “When the root is established, the Way is born.” “A man who is filial and fraternal, yet fond of offending his superiors, is rare indeed; one who is not fond of offending his superiors, yet fond of creating disorder, has never existed.” From self to family, from family to state, from state to all under Heaven—this is a continuous path. The *Doctrine of the Mean* takes the five relationships as “the universal Way under Heaven.”

27. A group is a “reality” beyond the individual, but it is not a tangible thing. We cannot point to some concrete object with physical form and say, “This is the group.” It is a bundle of relations among persons; it is a force that controls the behavior of each individual; it is something on which the lives of its members depend; it is a common will that precedes any individual and yet cannot exist apart from individuals.

28. The “agent,” or minister, is a basic concept in a society organized according to the group mode of association. The clergyman who carries out the will of God is a minister; the official who carries out the authority of the group is also a minister. Both are “agents,” not God or the group itself. The distinction between God and the clergyman, between the state and the government, must not be confused.

29. The social sphere extended outward from the self is made up of one strand after another of personal ties, and each strand is maintained by a moral element. The social sphere is pushed outward from the “self,” and in this process of extension there are various routes. The most fundamental is kinship: parent-child and sibling relations. The corresponding moral elements are filial piety and fraternal deference.

30. The parent-child relation is bilateral, referring to both father and mother; children are limited to those born of the married pair. This social group is joined together for the birth and rearing of children. In societies where the task of bearing and rearing children is borne by individuals, such a social group will not be lacking.
